Study E: the invariant itself -- how well can the bandwidth be measured?
  the fingerprint in study D is 'this emitter is 1 MHz wide'. With
  1.953 MHz bins the answer can only ever be a multiple of 1.953 MHz,
  so the question is whether 1 MHz can be told from 3 or 6 MHz at all
  true BW        bins estimated BW correct at 20/10/0 dB
  1 MHz             1      1.95 MHz    100/100/100 %
  2 MHz             1      1.95 MHz    100/100/100 %
  3 MHz             2      3.91 MHz    100/100/100 %
  6 MHz             3      5.86 MHz    100/100/100 %
  10 MHz            5      9.76 MHz    100/100/100 %
  The invariant is quantised by the bin width: 1, 2, 3, 6 and 10 MHz land
  on 1, 1, 2, 3 and 5 bins -- so 1 and 2 MHz are *the same fingerprint*
  on the shipped channelizer, while 1 versus 6 MHz is unambiguous at
  every SNR measured. A finer
  transform (4096-point, 488 kHz bins) is what a 1-versus-2 MHz
  fingerprint would need -- the same lever as the delay resolution in
  V07 section 13.3.
  Hop-to-hop phase coherence is *not* needed for any of this: the
  estimator uses inter-element phase only, so a coherent hopper and an
  independent one are measured identically. Coherence would add a
  fingerprint (the phase law across hops), never accuracy.
