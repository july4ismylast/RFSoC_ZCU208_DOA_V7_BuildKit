Study I: what the leading-edge delay is worth to the section 13 ML
  emitter and coherent repeater 29 deg apart, equal power; the delay
  comes from the leading edge (half a sample at 2 GS/s):
  bandwidth      bins   both < 3 deg      delay error
  1 MHz             1             0%         0.5 ns

  20 MHz           10             0%         5.0 ns
  50 MHz           26           100%         0.5 ns
  50 MHz           26             0%         5.0 ns
  The delay does not have to be perfect -- with the relative phase left
  free (the correct model) it only sets the slope across the band, so
  the tolerance is about 1/(10*bandwidth): 2 ns at 5 MHz, 0.5 ns at
  50 MHz. Half a sample from the leading edge covers both. What it
  cannot do is create frequency diversity: at 1 MHz the whole signal is
  one bin and the pair stays unsolvable on four elements.

VERDICT
  A radar transmits, so the decoy must receive first: the repeat is
  always later than the direct path by at least its turnaround. That is
  a physics guarantee, not a heuristic, and it is what this scenario
  adds to section 13.
  The leading edge therefore holds the radar alone, and its DOA is the
  answer -- 0.79 deg error at 20 ns of window against 14.08 deg for the
  whole-pulse method.
  Shortening the window costs SNR, not truth: 20 ns at 0 dB per sample
  is enough, and the window only has to stay inside the decoy's dead
  time.
  The edge delay makes the section 13 two-path ML usable without a
  blind search: 100% at 50 MHz, 100% at 20 MHz, 100% at 5 MHz and
  0% at 1 MHz, where one bin carries no frequency diversity at all.
  What still fails, and must be said out loud:
   (a) a continuous-wave source has no leading edge -- section 13's
       rungs are all that is left, with their measured limits;
   (b) the window needs an edge-detection time reference; the 2 ns case
       above is the sensitivity, not a null result;
   (c) a decoy that repeats from *the same direction* is now separable
       in time (that is the point), but a decoy that also arrives first
       is physically impossible only as long as it must hear the pulse;
       an active decoy that generates its own look-alike waveform from a
       library breaks the ordering and falls back to section 13 rungs.
  [PASS] the 20 ns gate is within 1 deg while the whole-pulse
         method is more than 2 deg off.
  [PASS] over-running the gate past the repeat (W = 2*tau) is
         measurably worse than W = tau.
  [PASS] 20 ns at 0 dB per sample is enough (RMSE 0.52 deg).
  [PASS] the tail gives the decoy's own direction: 39.6 deg for a
         decoy at 40 deg, over a 100 ns tail.
  [PASS] the gate holds under every waveform: worst 0.39 deg
         (LFM 0.39, soft-edged LFM 0.37, tan-FM 0.12, Barker 0.24).
  [PASS] the tail gives the decoy direction under every waveform
         (worst 0.5 deg).
  [PASS] with the +/-0.5 dB / +/-5 deg residual the gate stays under
         3 deg at 50 and 100 ns (worst 2.29 deg); a 10 ns window is
         noise-limited at 3.2 deg, which is a dwell choice, not a
