  Holding the last direction is what §15 did, and it is not silly: it
  keeps 98% of the mover in one piece at 0.5 deg/frame and 73% at
  1 deg/frame, which is the measurement noise -- and then falls apart: at
  16 deg the mover becomes 7.0 pieces and only 14% stays together.
  where the mover becomes 7.0 pieces and only 14% of it stays in
  one track.
  Prediction carries the same mover with 1.5 pieces and 89% in one
  track at 16 deg/frame. Prediction alone is not enough (7.2 pieces):
  with no history the predicted direction is the last one, so the first
  step still has to pass the gate. Opening that gate for the first two
  frames is what makes acquisition work, and it costs nothing after.

Study M2: acquisition -- how open must the first gate be?
  a mover at a fixed step, 8 frames, both rules with prediction; the
  first-gate width is swept. The number is the mover's pieces (1 =
  acquired in one track).
  step           gate 4    gate 8   gate 16   gate 20
  4 deg             2.2       1.4       1.2       1.2
  8 deg             7.6       2.4       1.5       1.1
  16 deg            6.5       7.4       2.8       1.4
  The first gate has to be at least the step plus a few times the
  per-frame noise -- 16 deg for a 16 deg/frame mover -- and it only
  applies to the first two frames of a track; after that the measured
  rate carries the gate back to 4 deg.

Study M3: a manoeuvre, and what carries identity through it
  T1 1 MHz hopping, +5 deg/frame for 5 frames then -5 deg/frame for 5
  (a 10 deg/frame change). T2 1 MHz CW at +40 deg -- SAME bandwidth, so
  only cadence can name them. 4 deg gate, prediction on, first gate open.
  with a 4 deg gate and a 10 deg/frame turn: 3.1 tracks for two
  emitters, 2.1 pieces of the mover, 100% of its detections still in
  the right identity.
  The geometry breaks at the turn -- the prediction is wrong by twice the
  step -- and the track is re-acquired within a frame or two because the
  gate that re-acquires it is scored on cadence as well: 100% of the
  mover's detections stay with the mover and none go to the CW emitter
  that shares its bandwidth. That is the point of the invariants: the
  angle failed, the identity did not.

Study M4: two movers crossing each other
  T1 from -30 deg at +5 deg/frame, T2 from +30 deg at -5 deg/frame,
  10 frames: they meet at 0 deg around frame 6. Both 1 MHz hoppers, same
  bandwidth, equal mean power, 4 dB rms of per-frame fading.
  rule                               tracks    swapped  final error
  last direction                       12.2         0%     13.70 deg
  predicted, first gate open            2.6         0%      1.27 deg
  Prediction lands within 1.3 deg of the truth at the end and keeps
  100% of runs on the right identity; the last-direction rule needs
  12.2 tracks and misses by 13.7 deg. A crossing is not automatically a
  swap. These two are inside the 4 deg gate of each other for a single
  frame, and one frame of ambiguity is resolved by the rate that each
  track has already measured. The 15-30 % swaps of V07 14.6 came from a
  decoy that stayed close for tens of hops and manoeuvred; that is the
  case where only a multi-hypothesis tracker helps.

VERDICT
  Correct objection: for a moving emitter the direction is not a key.
  It becomes a prediction. The identity of a mover is carried by
  continuity (a bounded angular rate), bandwidth and cadence -- measured
  here, in that order of usefulness.
  1. associating on the last direction is what a static-target
     tracker does; it survives 1.0 deg/frame and turns the mover into
     7.0 pieces at 16 deg/frame (M1).
  2. predicting the direction from the measured rate carries the same
     mover with 1.5 piece at 16 deg/frame (M1), provided the first
     gate is opened to the maximum angular speed for two frames (M2:
     1.4 pieces with a 20 deg first gate against 6.5 with 4 deg).
  3. a manoeuvre breaks the prediction (a 10 deg/frame turn leaves the
     track at 2.1 pieces) and identity is re-established from
     bandwidth and cadence: 100% of the mover's detections stay with
     it, none go to the equal-bandwidth emitter standing beside it (M3).
  4. two movers crossing swap in 0% of runs when both keep a
     constant velocity and fade; a decoy that manoeuvres through the
     crossing is the V07 14.6 case, where prediction only softens it.
  So the cluster key for a moving emitter is not a direction and not a
  fingerprint: it is (bandwidth, cadence, continuity), with the
  direction and its rate as the state you predict. Masking appears
  nowhere in it.
